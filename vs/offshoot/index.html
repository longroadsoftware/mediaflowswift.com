MediaFlowSwift vs OffShoot: Which One Fits Your Workflow?

Both prove a copy is good with a checksum, not just a successful drag. OffShoot is built for fast, large-scale transfers across many sources and destinations at once, with S3 and scripting in its Pro tier. MediaFlowSwift is built for what happens after the copy: reviewing, organizing, archiving and publishing the footage, not just moving it.

What does each app actually verify?

MediaFlowSwift computes a SHA-256 checksum of every file at import, then reads the copy back in full and compares it — the hash function set out in the US government's own Secure Hash Standard. It checks again, from scratch, right before Clear Card deletes anything from the card. OffShoot performs verified transfers using checksums to and from any type of storage medium, and can write an MHL sidecar log of what it checked, in the format kept by the American Society of Cinematographers’ ASC Media Hash List initiative. MediaFlowSwift keeps its own record of what was checked inside the project rather than as a separate MHL file.

What does OffShoot do better?

OffShoot multiplies sources by destinations: point it at several cards and several drives, and it runs every source-to-destination pair as one job, which matters on a set with more than one DIT station. Its Pro edition adds S3 transfers, scripting and APIs, and support for high-end cameras such as the Alexa 35 — the kind of setup a dedicated DIT or a post house would ask for. It also has a free companion app, Connect, for watching a transfer's progress from a browser, and it runs on Windows as well as Mac.

What does MediaFlowSwift do better?

OffShoot's job ends once the files have arrived and been checked. MediaFlowSwift keeps going: it categorizes each clip into folders by camera and category as it copies, offers frame-accurate review with star ratings and tags, archives to numbered drives with a record of what went where, and writes PDF, HTML and CSV reports for an editor. Studio Pro also drafts YouTube titles, descriptions, chapters and tags from the finished video and can set them on the upload for you. If a card going safely into one tidy project folder matters more than moving data across a fleet of drives, that is the gap.

Which one should you pick?

If a production already has a DIT moving large volumes across several drives, archiving to S3, or working with cameras like the Alexa 35, OffShoot's Pro tier is built for exactly that. If what you actually want is one card to turn into one organized, reviewed, reported project without juggling a separate app for the rest of the pipeline, that is what MediaFlowSwift is for.
