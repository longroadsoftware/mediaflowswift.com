MediaFlowSwift vs Just Using Finder and a Spreadsheet

Finder and a spreadsheet cost nothing and need no new app. What they do not do is prove a copy is safe: a drag-and-drop that finishes without an error can still leave you with a damaged file, and nothing tells you that until you try to use it. MediaFlowSwift's checksum checks exist specifically to catch that case.

Why isn't a successful copy in Finder proof enough?

Dragging files in Finder, or using a card reader's built-in copy, moves the bytes and tells you when it's done. It does not compute a checksum of the original and compare it against the checksum of the copy, so it cannot tell the difference between a perfect copy and one with a few flipped bits. A file with dropped data can still have the right name, the right size, and still open and even play for a few seconds before it stutters or goes black. The hash functions that catch this — such as SHA-256, set out in the US government's own Secure Hash Standard — are not something Finder's Copy command runs.

What does a spreadsheet miss that an app like this keeps track of?

A spreadsheet can record which card went where, but it only knows what you remember to type into it, and it does not look inside your drives to check that what it says is still true. It will not notice when a drive is renamed, a folder gets moved, or a copy that was supposed to finish actually stopped partway through. MediaFlowSwift keeps that record automatically: every clip's status — on the card, in a working copy, at the project's destination, or archived — is read from the files themselves, not typed in by hand, and a copy that fails its check is never marked as if it succeeded.

When is the manual way good enough?

For a handful of clips you are about to edit the same day, from a card you are not in a hurry to reuse, Finder is genuinely fine — the risk of a bad copy going unnoticed is low, and the cost of checking by eye is small. The manual way gets riskier as the stakes go up: more cards, more projects, footage you cannot reshoot, or a card you need to format and hand back today. That is the point where an unverified copy becomes an expensive mistake rather than an inconvenience.

What does MediaFlowSwift add, and what does it cost?
