What’s new in MediaFlowSwift

MediaFlow takes your footage from the card to the finished upload, and keeps every clip safe along the way. Here is what each new version brings.

MediaFlowSwift offers each new version itself, with what is new in it. You can also get the newest one from the download page.

1.10.48 October 11, 2026 Safer archives, faster Free Up Space, and privacy and licence promises kept to the letter. 5 changes
  • Two projects with the same name, or two drives that look alike, are never mixed up. Archiving a project to a drive that already holds another project with the same name now puts yours beside it, as “Trip (2)”, and never replaces the other one. Restoring checks that each drive is the very one your project was archived to, and that the folder on it is your project’s, so a lookalike drive or another project’s folder is never used by mistake. If the wrong drive is connected, MediaFlow tells you which one it needs, and changes nothing until you connect it.
  • Free up space without reading every copy from your drive again. MediaFlow now remembers which copies it read back in full when it organized them. When you free up space, it asks once whether to check those copies again or use that earlier check, and tells you how much checking again would read from your drive: a big saving over a phone connection. Copies it never fully checked are always read again, and the copies on your Mac are still checked every time. Staged files now wait in a Cleanup folder on your Mac instead of being uploaded to your drive first.
  • Place names keep your exact spot private, just as Settings promises. When you turn on place names, MediaFlow now rounds your clips’ coordinates to about 110 m before asking Apple for the name, so the exact spot where you filmed is never sent for place names. Settings › Privacy and Help now say exactly what is sent.
  • Working away from home? Know the one drive setting that can lock a file. A new Help page explains your network drive’s “durable handles” setting: why it helps on a steady home network, why over a phone, a hotspot or a VPN it can leave a file locked for hours, and when to turn it off. Find it under Setup & Network, or search Help for “durable handles”.
  • Your licence key stays put, even if the licence service has a bad moment. If the licence service ever says it doesn’t recognise your key, MediaFlow now keeps the key and your plan keeps working while it checks again, just as it does when you’re offline. Only a signed answer from the service can end a licence, so you’ll never have to look up your key again because of a hiccup on our side.

1.10.41 October 5, 2026 Your plan is protected from copies, saves never get stuck when your server connection drops, and drive numbers never clash. 6 changes
  • Your plan can't be faked or copied. MediaFlowSwift now believes only an answer the licence service has signed for your key on your Mac, so nobody can hand-write a plan or borrow another Mac's. The first time you open this version, it checks your licence once; if you're offline then, your plan keeps working for three days while it waits for a connection. If your Mac's clock is far off, Settings › Plan says so and tells you how to fix it.
  • A save no longer gets stuck if your server connection drops mid-save. If your database server restarted or the network dropped while MediaFlow was saving, that save could wait forever, and every save after it waited too. Now MediaFlow lets go of it within 15 seconds (at once if the server says it is closing), connects again and saves your change once more, or keeps it to sync as soon as the server is back. Nothing you changed is lost.
  • Every new archive drive gets a number of its own, even when two Macs set one up at once. When two Macs shared one database, setting up new archive drives at the same moment could give both the same number, so one drive couldn't be recorded, or two drives ended up sharing a number. Now MediaFlow claims the number for your drive before writing it on the drive, so no other Mac can take it, and your drives keep counting up as before.
  • A project you emptied stays in your library. If you had removed every clip from a project still called "Untitled Project" or "Test", MediaFlow could take it for an unused leftover and delete it the next time it connected to your database, along with its record of what you removed. Now a project is only tidied away when it truly holds nothing, so your removed clips are always on record.
  • MediaFlow notices when your database server goes away, and still rides out a slow connection. If the server restarts or the network drops while MediaFlow is checking on it, MediaFlow used to wait for an answer that never came, and never noticed it had lost the connection. Now, once the server has said nothing at all for 15 seconds, it connects again, just as it does after any other disconnect. A slow link that is still working, over a phone or satellite connection, is left to finish what it is doing, and anything cut off is saved again once you're back.
  • Category Suggestions now show the whole reason. Each suggestion's reason used to be cut off mid-word with no way to read the rest. Now it wraps under the clip, so you can see exactly why MediaFlow suggests a category, and the category name stays on one line with its buttons neatly lined up.
