Privacy

Last updated 13 October 2026. “We” and “us” on this page mean Long Road Software LLC, which makes MediaFlowSwift. This page describes the app; it says the same as Settings › Privacy inside MediaFlowSwift, which is the authoritative list and changes with the app.

The short version

MediaFlowSwift works on your Mac and your own network. It has no account and collects no usage data or analytics. Your media, projects and database stay on your Mac and on your network. It contacts us for two things only: to ask whether there is a newer version, and, once you have entered a licence key, to check that key. Both are described below.

A few features need a service run by another company. Each one is off until you turn it on in Settings › Privacy, which shows what is sent, to whom, and what for. You can turn any of them off again at any time.

What the app asks us

ConnectionWhenWhat is sent
Checking for a new versionShortly after launch, then every few hours (every half hour while the site cannot be reached)A request for one small file that names the newest version; nothing about you or your Mac goes with it. Installing an update downloads the new copy from this site and checks it is exactly the file promised and signed by us.
Licence checkNothing until you enter a licence key. Then when you enter it, once a day after that, when you click Check now, and when you release a MacEntering the key sends the key, a one-way code made from this Mac’s hardware id (the same for every user account on this Mac, and never the hardware id itself), and the Mac’s name as you see it in System Settings. The daily check, Check now and release send the key and that code only. Until an account first activated by an earlier version of the app has moved to that code, its checks, and Release this Mac, also send the random id that version made for it, so that its place on the key can be freed. The answer is your plan, when it renews or ends, and which Macs hold seats. No email, no account name, nothing about your projects. The key and the answer are kept in your Keychain. The licence service keeps the key, the plan, Paddle’s ids for the purchase, a one-way hash of the buying email, and for each Mac the code it checks in with, the Mac’s name and when it last checked in.

What stays on your network

  • Your network storage: finding it, connecting to it, reading and writing media, and the shared database file.
  • Your database server, if you use one. That connection is not encrypted, so keep the server on a network you trust.
  • A local model you point at another computer on your network receives the same frames or text as above and nothing more; an address on the internet is refused.

Problem reports sent to us

If you send a problem report to us, it is filed as an issue in our development tracker so that we can fix the fault. It contains only what the app showed you before you clicked Send. If you chose to include an email address for a reply, we use it for that reply and nothing else. We keep reports for as long as the fault is being worked on and for our release records afterwards.

This website

This site and longroadsoftware.com are static pages served by GitHub Pages. We use Google Analytics on both to count visits and see which pages people read and how they found us. It sets cookies in your browser and sends Google the page address, the site that linked you here, your browser and device type, and your approximate location, under Google’s privacy policy. We do not send it your name, email or licence key, and it does not run on the thank-you or key pages. You can block it with any content blocker or with Google’s opt-out add-on; the site works the same without it. GitHub, as the host, keeps ordinary server logs of requests (IP address, page requested, time) under GitHub’s privacy statement. Fonts are loaded from Google Fonts, which receives the request for the font files.

Purchases

Payment is handled by Paddle, our merchant of record: the checkout, your card and billing details, receipts and tax are theirs, under Paddle’s privacy policy. Paddle tells our licence service about the purchase so that a key can be made; the thank-you page fetches that key with the transaction number. We never see card numbers. Paddle.js, the checkout script, loads on the home page only.
