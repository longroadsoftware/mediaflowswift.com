Who makes MediaFlowSwift, and why

MediaFlowSwift is made by Long Road Software LLC, a small company in Tennessee that publishes the app and supports it directly. The person behind it is Mike, a video creator who wrote the app for his own footage and runs his own channel on it.

Where it came from

Mike also makes On Island Time, a YouTube channel about living and travelling in an RV. Every trip comes home as cards full of clips, and for years those clips went onto whatever drive had room, and the drives went onto a shelf. The shelves filled faster than any record of what was on which drive, and every time a card was cleared there was the same quiet question: was the copy really there?

MediaFlowSwift began as the tool to fix that. It records where every clip is — on a card, in a working copy, at the project’s destination, or on a numbered archive drive — copies footage into folders ready for editing, and clears space only once the copy has been read back and proved to match. It is sold to other creators because the problem is not special to one channel: anyone who shoots more than fits on one drive ends up with the same shelf.

What it promises about your files

The promise is narrow, and it is kept literally. Whenever MediaFlowSwift copies footage and then removes the original — clearing a card, freeing up space after Organize, moving a project to an editing drive and back — the copy is checked against the original first, and if the two do not match, nothing is removed. Your footage stays in ordinary folders that any app can read, on the drives and network shares you chose. The app has no account and collects no usage data; apart from checking for updates and checking your licence key, it talks to no outside service until you turn one on, and Settings › Privacy lists what each one sends. When something fails, it says which file, why, and what to do. That is the whole promise: not that drives never fail, but that nothing is removed on a guess.
